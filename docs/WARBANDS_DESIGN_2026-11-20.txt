CONFIRMED REQUIREMENT

One CTA warband UUID coordinates several independent NORMAL WoW parties, never
a raid. An organizer advertises a warband; accepting the CTA listing establishes
logical membership. Native party invitations and their acceptance are a separate
step. This supersedes the earlier suggestion to ship one party per warband.
The first testing build bounds a warband to four parties / 20 CTA members.

Current flow: solo/party leader starts a listing; other opted-in reachable CTA
players join it. Existing party leaders register their party first, then their
members join the same UUID. A solo recruit is assigned an available party, or
becomes the next party host when existing parties have no room. The assigned
host manually uses Invite to party; the recruit accepts through WoW. CTA never
leaves a native party, converts to raid, automatically invites or auto-accepts.
Non-CTA native party members occupy capacity but are not CTA members.

Discovery uses GUILD, the organizer's normal PARTY and explicit named WHISPER
subscriptions. Default OFF every session. At most eight named watches/subscribers;
joined CTA members receive direct coordinator updates independently of that cap.
Registering a prefix does not discover everyone running CTA. A faction-wide or
all-server directory is not implemented. Public zone discovery remains future work.

WHAT IS IMPLEMENTED

WarbandProtocol.lua: bounded wire parsing, names, sender-key normalization,
UUID generation, scope and freshness checks. Warbands.lua: local state store,
queues, discovery, membership leases, group assignments, role checks and lifecycle.
WarbandUI.lua: listings/details, manual party-invite controls and native pin art.
Map.lua reuses CTA's native map provider and optional OpenMap transforms.
No generic event framework or external service is needed for this slice.

An organizer owns the listing, recruitment state and follow/rally position.
Members send their own native-party summary and position to that organizer.
Only the registered party host supplies that group's advertised location.
The coordinator sends a compact snapshot of up to four group-host positions.
Ordinary members' individual positions are not forwarded by this protocol.
Joining consents to send your position/party summary to the organizer. Group-host
positions and reported counts are shared with the listing's audience. Member-only
numbered map markers are a UI choice, not a confidentiality boundary.

One 28x28 crossed axe/sword/banner marker advertises potential activity, matching
the report-pin footprint. Numbered 16x16 group markers are shown to members.
The runtime draws native lines, avoiding unsupported file texture assumptions.
assets/warband-pin.svg and PNG previews remain editable design references.
assets/warband-pin-runtime-preview.png illustrates the actual native-line geometry
at 28px and 4x, rendered offline; it is not a WoW screenshot.
A rally is a fixed point selected with /cta warband rally; follow publishes the
organizer's moving location. Neither is a measured center of all members.
Counts are explicitly organizer/host-reported, not confirmed combat participants.
No warband data generates reports, heat or city-raid authorization.

PROTOCOL CTA_WB1

Tab-separated version 1 packets, at most 255 bytes and 20 fields. UUIDs are
canonical lowercase v4-shaped identifiers, generated from WoW random bytes mixed
with readable identity/time and a persisted counter without reseeding shared RNG.
They identify objects; they are not cryptographic credentials or numeric layers.
A fresh coordinator session UUID is created at login/reload; each warband has its
own UUID. Each new member enrollment has another UUID so old leave/update packets
cannot affect a later enrollment. Invitation retries preserve that enrollment
and its update sequence. Listings and memberships are not resumed after reload.

A / G / E: organizer advertisement, group snapshot, terminal end. Envelope:
version, kind, warband UUID, coordinator session UUID, sequence, updatedAt,
expiresAt, faction, ruleset, region. A then includes revision, map, x/y scaled to
10000, forming/roaming/rallying, recruiting, follow/rally, CTA count and title.
G appends up to four base36 rows: slot,map,x,y,nativeCount,sampleAgeSeconds.
G preserves source sample age, so coordinator rebroadcast does not freshen stale
positions. E contains only the envelope. A/G sequences are checked separately
so route reordering does not discard a complete sibling snapshot; E also checks
the highest sequence observed across both. An accepted END cannot be revived.

S / X: subscribe/unsubscribe a known organizer. J / R: join request/result bound
to the current coordinator, UUID/session and request nonce. I: coordinator asks
a registered host to display a manual invitation task. U: member-only update,
including enrollment UUID and increasing sequence. L: leave that enrollment.
K: member lease renewal from the coordinator, tied to the enrollment and an
already-sent update sequence. It is not an acknowledgement of every snapshot.
Remote test control CTA_CTL1 is independent and grants no warband authority.

Timing/bounds:
- Organizer state: minimum 15 seconds when dirty/moving, 30 seconds when idle.
- Members: update every 15 seconds while enrolled and readable outdoors.
- Lease replies: at most once per member per 30 seconds; membership TTL 90s.
- Listing and group-source TTL: 90s; future timestamp tolerance: five seconds.
- Join/reply/request freshness: 30s; no automatic join/invite retry.
- Named subscriptions renew every 60s; organizer subscription TTL 120s.
- Send attempts: at most four per second, including rejected sends.
- Queue: 96 entries, coalesced by type/listing/destination, discard after 25s.
  Coalescing preserves queue order; control/lease/end messages have priority.
  Under saturation some intermediate snapshots are replaced by newer ones.
- Receive budgets: eight-message burst/two per second per sender and a global
  forty-message burst/eight per second. Control requests additionally limited
  to one per five seconds per sender. No retransmission storm or recursive relay.
- At most 64 listings, 256 replay histories and 128 sender/owner entries.
  Replay/owner history expires after 190s, beyond maximum accepted packet age.

AUTHORITY, LIBRARIES AND LIMITS

Everything running in the live addon uses WoW's Lua environment and public game
APIs. Lua libraries can be embedded: AceComm provides communication/fragmentation,
ChatThrottleLib outgoing throttling, LibSerialize table serialization. They do not
provide application-specific authority or prove that a client tells the truth.
This release adds no dependency: fixed bounded packets fit one native message,
a small explicit parser suffices, and CTA owns its bounded queue/state checks.
Consider a shared ChatThrottleLib scheduler if traffic grows; fragmentation or
serialization should be added only when there is a concrete data-shape need.

Obfuscation is not authentication. Every client is editable, so a shared secret
embedded in Lua is recoverable. Changing the prefix or encoding bytes cannot
prevent forged contents. Never execute received Lua or deserialize executable
objects. This protocol has no loadstring, arbitrary remote execution, compression
bomb surface or external Python/C# runtime bridge.

Authority comes from the sender supplied by CHAT_MSG_ADDON, route, known listing
owner/session, enrollment, nonce/sequence and bounded freshness. Unknown whisper
advertisements are ignored unless explicitly subscribed/joining/enrolled. PARTY
advertisements additionally require a currently resolvable native party leader.
Existing UUIDs cannot be overwritten by another sender or session. A publisher
can maintain one listing; replacement must be newer and rate-limited, and prior
known listings are closed. Malformed, oversized, stale, cross-scope, wrong-role
and duplicate traffic cannot update accepted state or indefinitely renew expiry.

This is cooperation with bounded trust, not server-authoritative anti-cheat.
An authorized organizer can lie about its own count/location; an enrolled member
can lie about its own party observations. Scope fields from remote clients are
claims where native roster evidence is unavailable. A malicious client can mint
its own new listing. Sender validation cannot prove a fight, faction, leadership
outside a readable roster or same-layer presence. Rate limits bound local work;
they cannot guarantee service under a coordinated flood. No encryption or private
location transport is claimed. Trusted-key enrollment/server attestation would
need a separate trust design and still could not attest an untrusted observation.

LAYER AND LIFECYCLE

CTA shows waiting for native party, in assigned party/co-location unverified,
world refresh pending, or host position unavailable. It does not infer a numeric
layer from NPC GUIDs and never promises the leader's layer wins. Use Blizzard's
normal grouping/refresh flow and the existing three-player diagnostic. Matching
coordinates, addon messages and membership are not proof of mutual visibility.
Each separate party needs its own check against a stationary encounter anchor.
The automated reciprocal-observation/arrival-verdict protocol is not implemented.

Coordinator leadership loss, raid conversion or instance entry ends the warband.
A member entering a raid/instance leaves CTA membership on its next update.
A departed subgroup host retires that slot until its members leave; there is no
silent reassignment or automatic leader election. Those players leave/rejoin
through a current party leader to establish a new group. Ending/leaving CTA never
changes the actual WoW party. Lost END/leave packets fall back to bounded expiry.
Scope changes disable sharing and clear local warband state. Logout/reload has
best-effort withdrawal; recipient expiry remains necessary.

VALIDATION AND NEXT GATES

Eight offline Lua/mock suites pass for 0.6.0. The warband network test uses
independent clients, four full parties, 20 memberships and eight cross-guild
listeners while hosts move. It covers opt-in/identity/authority, nonce/session
replays, cancel/rejoin/invite retries, source positions, map-pin dimensions,
packet limits, throttle failures, full capacity, leases, END/expiry and raid exit.
These are offline results, not live WoW acceptance or a rendering screenshot.

Run WARBAND_TEST_0.6.0.txt, then the three-player layer diagnostic. Remaining MVP
work includes public discovery, calibrated cross-party arrival observations,
reliable PvP participation evidence and the preexisting live report/UI gates.
Remote warband verbs affect test fixtures only. No remote live-party assembly
or automatic native grouping is included.
